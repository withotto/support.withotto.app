Before he starts his reconciliation work for a client, Otto is shown examples of previous reconciliations for that client. We give him the direction on what information he should look at so he can understand how to tell _why_ that bank transaction matched the bill or invoice.

This initial training only helps Otto understand what makes a correct match, but not how to tell if something is not a match. We create synthetic non-matches so he can make good decisions, but he learns better from real examples. This is where feedback comes in: Otto will record his decisions every time he performs bank reconciliation for a client, and you can let him know if he was right or wrong.

## Understanding Otto's decisions

Each line Otto looked at has a card on the client's [reconciliation report](/bank-rec/portal/reconciliation-report/).

The table compares the bank statement transaction with the possible match from Xero. Otto's decision is in two places:

1. The status at the top of the card, "Reconciled" in this case. Where there's a reason, it follows the status.
2. Otto's score for the item being the match: 98% in this case. The figure after "needs" is the score he must reach to reconcile it.

### What actions can Otto take?

Each heading below shows a card's status, then its reason.

#### Reconciled

There was a single possible match and it matches the bank statement transaction.

When SmartMatch is in monitoring mode, the card shows "Monitoring: Would reconcile" instead. Otto records his decision but doesn't reconcile the transaction.

#### Skipped: Multiple possible matches, left for you to check

Xero offered more than one possible match, so Otto left the transaction for you to check, whatever his score. The card shows the match he thinks is right. Mark it as a match and he'll reconcile it on his next run as a free [GuidedMatch](/bank-rec/guides/guidedmatch/). From December 2026, the list under the card shows [every possible match](/bank-rec/guides/feedback/#when-xero-offers-several-possible-matches) Xero offered, so you can mark a different one.

When SmartMatch is in monitoring mode, the card shows "Monitoring: Would leave multiple possible matches for you to check" instead. Marking a match there records it for his next training, but he doesn't reconcile it while he's in monitoring mode. If the transaction is still unreconciled when monitoring mode ends, he reconciles your match on his next run, as long as Xero still offers it.

Until 9 November 2026, Otto reconciled these himself when he was confident enough, so older reports can show "Reconciled: Multiple possible matches".

#### Reconciled: GuidedMatch

You marked this as a match in the portal, so Otto reconciled it on his next run.

#### Skipped: Score below the required level

Otto's score for the possible match was below the [confidence](/bank-rec/guides/smartmatch/#confidence) the client requires, so he didn't reconcile it. Reports from before 9 November 2026 can also show "Skipped: Multiple possible matches, score below the required level", where Xero offered more than one and none reached it.

#### Skipped: Left for you after “Not a match”

You marked what Otto reconciled for this transaction, or a match he picked for it, as not a match. He leaves the transaction for you from then on, to avoid making a mistake. If the card now shows a different match and it's the right one, you can still mark it as a match. From December 2026, this also covers a possible match you ruled out in the [list of possible matches](/bank-rec/guides/feedback/#when-xero-offers-several-possible-matches) that later became his pick, and you can mark the right one as a match in the list.

#### Skipped: Your match is no longer available in Xero

You marked a match for this transaction, but when Otto next checked, Xero no longer offered it, so he couldn't reconcile it. That usually means it had been reconciled against another transaction or deleted. When a colleague marked the match, the card names them instead of "Your".

### Lines with nothing to answer

Some lines show as a single row under the cards, with the reason in place of Otto's decision:

- **Possible match dated after the bank transaction:** every possible match Xero offered is dated after the bank statement transaction, so Otto didn't score them. One can still be right, so check it and reconcile it in Xero yourself if it is.
- **Batch payment:** one payment covers several bills or invoices, which Otto doesn't reconcile.

## Giving feedback

You can tell Otto whether the items are a match or not using the buttons on the card and, from December 2026, on each possible match in the list under it. This is always used to improve his performance when he is next trained but can also cause other things to happen. See [feedback](/bank-rec/guides/feedback/) for more information.
